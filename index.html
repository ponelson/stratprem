How the math works

Total ARR drives the standalone SaaS value (Motion 1). Only the Cisco-attached subset of ARR drags Cisco hardware, Splunk, and services. Each attach ratio is annual Cisco revenue per $1 of Cisco-attached ARR. Multiplying by Cisco-attached ARR gives Cisco's annual revenue stream from that category, then translated into EV by an appropriate multiple. UCS and networking use the hardware EV multiple. Splunk has its own EV multiple slider (software economics — Cisco paid 7× ARR for Splunk in 2024; trades at a different multiple as a Cisco segment). Services is fixed at 1.5× (low-margin).

Motion 1 = Total ARR × SaaS multiple
Motion 2 = Motion 1 + Σ (Cisco-attached ARR × attachi × multiplei)
Drag premium = Motion 2 ÷ Motion 1

The model values gross dragged revenue, not gross margin. A more rigorous version would NPV the gross margin contribution at Cisco's WACC, since Cisco captures margin not revenue. Hardware streams also typically have follow-on revenue from refreshes (3–5 year cycles) and customer expansion not captured here — meaning the strategic premium in practice runs higher than this model shows.
